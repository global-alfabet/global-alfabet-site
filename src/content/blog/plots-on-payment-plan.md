To sell plots on a payment plan, show every buyer the same six numbers for each plot: the cash price, the price on the plan, the deposit, any fees paid upfront, the monthly payment with the number of months, and the total they will pay. Put them side by side for each option, say how they were worked out, and put the same numbers in the agreement. A buyer who can see the full cost can compare plans, budget for them and say yes with confidence.

## Why payment plans confuse buyers

Many buyers can't pay for a plot in one go, so a payment plan is often what makes the sale. But plans are easy to present badly:

- **Only the monthly figure is shown.** "From $780 a month" sounds affordable. The buyer doesn't see the deposit, the fees or the months until later.
- **The plan price is hidden.** Some developers charge more for a plot bought over time than for cash. Buyers should see both prices.
- **Fees appear late.** An agreement fee or admin fee that turns up at signing feels like a trick, even when it was always planned.
- **Every agent explains it differently.** One works it out on a phone calculator, another sends last year's price list.

Each one costs trust. The fix is to show the whole picture, the same way, for every plot.

## The six numbers to show for each plot

For every plot and every plan, show:

1. **Cash price.** What the plot costs if paid in full. If you price by size, also show the rate per square metre.
2. **Price on the plan.** If buying over time costs more, show the higher price and the rate behind it.
3. **Deposit.** As an amount, not only a percentage. "10% deposit" means little until the buyer sees the figure for their plot.
4. **Other upfront costs.** Agreement fees, admin fees, legal or transfer costs you charge: name each one and say when it is paid.
5. **Monthly payment and term.** The amount, how many months, and whether the payment is fixed.
6. **Total paid.** Deposit plus fees plus every monthly payment. It is the number that lets buyers compare plans, and some consumer credit rules ask lenders to give a single figure for the total cost of credit before signing.

Add one line on interest: whether the plan charges it, the yearly rate, and whether it can change. Longer terms mean lower monthly payments but more interest in total, so buyers need both figures to choose.

## A worked example

*Example figures only. They are not real prices or a real estate.*

A plot of 600 m² is priced at $60 per m² for cash and $65 per m² on a payment plan. The plan asks for a 10% deposit, a $500 agreement fee, a 3% admin fee on the balance, and charges 12% a year on the balance, repaid in fixed monthly payments.

- **Cash price:** 600 × $60 = $36,000. With the $500 agreement fee, the buyer pays **$36,500**.
- **Price on the plan:** 600 × $65 = $39,000.
- **Deposit:** 10% of $39,000 = $3,900.
- **Balance:** $39,000 − $3,900 = $35,100.
- **Admin fee:** 3% of $35,100 = $1,053.
- **Upfront in total:** $3,900 + $500 + $1,053 = **$5,453**.

The monthly payment depends on the term. At 12% a year (1% a month), with fixed payments:

- **Cash:** $36,500 in one payment. Total **$36,500**.
- **12 months:** $5,453 upfront, then $3,118.59 a month. Total **$42,876**.
- **36 months:** $5,453 upfront, then $1,165.82 a month. Total **$47,423**.
- **60 months:** $5,453 upfront, then $780.78 a month. Total **$52,300**.

Two things stand out. The 60-month plan has the lowest monthly payment, but it costs $15,800 more than cash. And the gap between the 36- and 60-month totals is almost $4,900. Shown like this, a buyer can decide whether the lower payment is worth it. Shown as "from $780 a month", they can't.

The monthly figure comes from the standard formula for a fixed-payment loan, where each payment covers some interest and some of the balance. Early payments are mostly interest; later ones pay off more of the balance. If you don't charge interest, the monthly payment is simply the balance divided by the months: $35,100 over 36 months is $975.

## Put it in writing

Whatever you show online, the agreement is what counts. Before a buyer signs, give them a written payment schedule with:

- **The plot**, by number and size, and the estate it is in.
- **Every amount and date:** the deposit, each fee, each monthly payment, and the final payment.
- **The interest rate**, and whether it can change.
- **What happens if a payment is late or missed:** any late fee, how many days' grace, and at what point the agreement can be cancelled.
- **What happens to money already paid** if the buyer cancels or the agreement ends.
- **When the buyer gets title or transfer,** and what they hold until then.
- **Whether they can pay early,** and whether early payment saves interest.
- **Whether the price is fixed** once the agreement is signed, even if you raise prices for later buyers.

The last points matter most. In some instalment sales the seller keeps legal title until the final payment, so a buyer who misses payments late in the plan can lose a great deal. Buyers deserve to know that before they sign, in plain words. Ask your lawyer to check your agreement against the rules where you sell. This is general information, not financial or legal advice.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **Advertising only the lowest monthly payment.** Show it with the deposit, the months and the total, or not at all.
- **Rounding that doesn't match.** If the table says $780.78 a month, the agreement shouldn't say $781.
- **Out-of-date price lists.** When prices change, old PDFs and screenshots keep circulating. Keep one current source and share links to it, not files.
- **Hiding tax.** Say whether prices include or exclude any sales tax, and keep it the same everywhere.
- **Promising what you can't keep.** "No interest" or "fixed for the whole term" must be true for every buyer, in writing.

## In short

**What should a payment plan for a plot show?**
The cash price, the price on the plan, the deposit, upfront fees, the monthly payment and number of months, and the total paid, for each option.

**How is the monthly payment worked out?**
With interest, a common method is a fixed payment that covers interest and part of the balance each month. Without interest, it is the balance divided by the number of months.

**What should go in the agreement?**
Every amount and date, the interest rate, what happens with late or missed payments, refunds, early payment, and when the buyer gets title.
