To launch a new phase of plots well, get five things ready before you announce it: a final layout plan and plot list that match, prices and payment terms in writing, honest information about services and approvals, a sales team and agents who all work from the same list, and one place where buyers can check what is still for sale. Then launch on a set day, and update every reservation and sale on the day it happens. Buyers who didn't buy in the last phase may be waiting for this one, so give them a clear reason to act.

## Why a new phase needs its own launch

A new phase is not a new development, but buyers treat it like one. They want to know which plots are new, what they cost, what is finished on site and what isn't, and how fast plots are going.

The risks are different too. Your team already has habits from the last phase: old price lists in their phones, plot numbers they know by heart, a way of holding plots for friends. Without a checklist, those habits carry over and cause the usual problems:

- **Two plans in circulation.** Buyers see the old phase plan and the new one, and can't tell which plots are which.
- **Old prices.** An agent quotes last year's rate because it's the list they have.
- **The same plot sold twice.** Two agents reserve the same plot on the same morning, because availability lives in two places.
- **Promises about services.** "Roads are coming" turns into a date nobody agreed.

Each of these costs trust, and trust is what sells the next phase too.

## 1. Check the plan and the plot list

Everything else is built on these two documents, so check them first.

- **Use the final layout plan for this phase.** It should come from your surveyor, show the phase boundary clearly, and carry a date or revision number. Keep the earlier phases on it, greyed out or labelled, so buyers can see where the new plots sit.
- **Make the plot list match the plan.** Every plot number on the plan should be on the list, with the same size. Check a few by hand against the plan, not only against last year's spreadsheet.
- **Number plots so they can't be confused.** If phase 1 used plots 1 to 120, don't start phase 2 at 1 again. Unique numbers keep reservations, agreements and enquiries clear.
- **Mark plots that are different.** Corner plots, plots next to open space, plots with a servitude or different building rules: note them on the list now, not when a buyer asks.

If buyers read the plan themselves, send them our guide on [how to read a layout plan before you buy a plot](/blog/how-to-read-a-layout-plan-before-you-buy-a-plot).

## 2. Set prices and terms in writing

Decide prices before launch day, not during it.

- **One price list for the phase.** Price per square metre by size, or a price per plot. Say whether prices include any tax, and when the list was issued.
- **Payment plans, written out.** If you offer them, show the deposit, the fees, the number of months, the monthly payment and the total for each option, the same way for every plot.
- **What "reserved" means.** How long a reservation holds a plot, whether it needs a deposit, and what happens when it lapses. Write it down and give it to every agent.
- **Launch prices, if you use them.** If early buyers get a lower price, say until when, or for how many plots, and keep to it.

Put the same numbers in the agreement. A buyer who sees one price online and another at signing won't trust the rest.

## 3. Be honest about what's on site

Buyers of a new phase often ask the same questions: are the roads in, is there water and power, when can I build? Answer them with what you know, and a date for each answer.

- **Services, phase by phase.** Roads, water, sewer and electricity: planned, under way or done. If something is planned, say so. Don't give a date you can't keep.
- **Approvals.** Know what you hold for this phase and when it was granted. Ask your lawyer what must be in place before you take deposits where you sell, and don't take them before it is.
- **Photos with dates.** Recent photos of the site, dated, show progress better than words. Use your own photos, not a picture of somewhere else.

This is general information, not legal advice. The rules for selling land differ from place to place.

## 4. Prepare what buyers will see

Decide where buyers will go when they hear about the phase, and make that one place complete.

- **One link for the phase.** A page or an interactive site plan with every plot, its size, its status and its price. Use it in every advert, post and message, so buyers always land on the current version.
- **A link to each plot.** When a buyer asks about one plot, send them straight to it.
- **A plot information pack.** One page per plot with its plan, size, boundary lengths, price and payment options, and who to contact. Buyers share these with family before they decide.
- **A short description of the phase.** How many plots, sizes from and to, what's on site, and how to reserve. Plain words, no promises you haven't checked.

If you're choosing between a PDF plan, a video and an interactive plan, our comparison of [what each format does well](/blog/interactive-site-plan-pdf-or-video) may help.

## 5. Brief your team and agents

Before launch day, sit down with everyone who will sell the phase.

- **Walk them through the plan.** Which plots are new, which are different, and how plot numbers work.
- **Give them the link, not files.** Price lists and screenshots go out of date the day you change a price. A link stays current.
- **Agree who changes status.** Name the people who can mark a plot reserved or sold, and ask them to do it on the same day.
- **Know where enquiries go.** Each agent should have their own link, so you know who brought each buyer. Our guide to [sharing plots with estate agents](/blog/share-plots-with-estate-agents) covers this in detail.
- **Agree the answers.** Write down the five questions buyers ask most, with the answers you want everyone to give.

## 6. Launch on a set day

Pick a day and tell people in advance. Buyers from earlier phases, people who enquired and didn't buy, and your agents' lists are the first audience.

On the day:

- **Check the link once more.** Every plot for sale marked for sale, every price right, the contact details working.
- **Send the same link everywhere.** Email, social media, adverts and your website.
- **Answer fast.** Enquiries on launch day are the warmest you'll get. Have someone ready to reply.

## 7. Keep it current after launch

The first weeks decide whether buyers trust your availability.

- **Update status the same day.** A plot reserved this morning should show as reserved this afternoon.
- **Release lapsed reservations.** If a reservation runs out, put the plot back on sale and tell the agents.
- **Retire old material.** When prices change, say so, and stop sending the old list.
- **Keep a record.** Note every enquiry, who it came from and what happened. It tells you which adverts and agents work, and it helps plan the next phase.

## Your checklist

Before you announce the phase, check that:

1. The layout plan is final, dated, and shows the phase boundary.
2. The plot list matches the plan, with no plot numbers shared with earlier phases.
3. Plots that are different are marked on the list.
4. There is one price list, with a date, and tax shown the same way everywhere.
5. Payment plans show the deposit, fees, months, monthly payment and total.
6. "Reserved" has a written meaning, including how long it lasts.
7. Services and approvals are described honestly, each with a date.
8. Buyers have one link for the phase, and a link to each plot.
9. Each plot has an information pack buyers can save and share.
10. Your team and agents are briefed, and know who changes status.
11. Each agent has their own link.
12. A launch day is set, and someone is ready to answer enquiries.

## In short

**What should I do before launching a new phase of plots?**
Check that the plan and plot list match, set prices and payment terms in writing, describe services and approvals honestly, brief your team and agents, and give buyers one link with every plot's status and price.

**How do I stop the same plot being sold twice?**
Keep availability in one place, agree who can change a plot's status, and update it on the day of every reservation and sale.

**How should I number plots in a new phase?**
Carry on from the last phase's numbers, so no two plots in the development share a number.
