Each format answers a different question. A printed or PDF layout plan is the accurate record: plot numbers, boundaries and sizes. A video flyover sells the feeling of the place in a minute. An interactive site plan lets a buyer find one plot and check its size, status and price for themselves. Most developers need more than one. The right mix depends on what your buyers still need to know before they decide, and on whether you can keep each format up to date.

## What do buyers need to decide?

Before a buyer pays a deposit, they need clear answers to a short list of questions:

1. **Which plot, exactly?** Its number and where it sits in the estate.
2. **Is it still for sale?** Today, not when the brochure was made.
3. **How big is it, and what shape?** Area, frontage and boundary lengths.
4. **What is around it?** Roads, open spaces, neighbouring plots, which way it faces.
5. **What will it cost?** The price, the deposit and the monthly payments.
6. **What could I build there?** A picture of a home on that plot.
7. **Is it real and approved?** The approved plan, and who to ask.
8. **Who do I talk to?** A person, reachable in one step.

A buyer who can visit the site answers several of these by walking it. A buyer in another city or another country can't. Whatever you send them has to do the work of the visit.

Keep that list in mind as we look at each format.

## The layout plan as a PDF or print

This is the format almost every estate starts with. Your surveyor or town planner draws it, and it is usually the plan the local authority approves. It is often shared as a PDF, printed on large sheets at the sales office, or sent as a photo of the printout.

**What it does well**

- **It is the record.** Plot numbers, boundaries, sizes and road reserves come from the survey. It answers questions 1, 3 and 7 better than anything else.
- **It travels anywhere.** It opens on any device, prints, and can be attached to an agreement or sent to a lawyer.
- **Nothing to keep running.** Once it is drawn, it costs nothing to share.

**Where it falls short**

- **It is hard to read on a phone.** Plot numbers are small, and the buyer has to zoom and scroll to find one.
- **It doesn't show status.** A plan shows every plot the same way. Sold and reserved plots need a separate list, and that list goes out of date the day a plot sells.
- **It has no sense of place.** It shows lines, not what standing on the plot would feel like.
- **Copies multiply.** Old versions stay in chats and inboxes, and a buyer may not know which is current.

## The video flyover

A video flyover is a short film of the estate from the air: drone footage of the land, or a 3D animation of the finished estate, or both, often with music and a voice-over.

**What it does well**

- **It sells the feeling.** In a minute, a buyer sees the setting, the roads and the open space, and starts to picture living there.
- **It is made for adverts and social media.** Video fits naturally into feeds, ads and messaging apps.
- **It needs nothing from the buyer.** Press play and watch.

**Where it falls short**

- **The camera decides what the buyer sees.** A buyer can't stop over plot 145 and look at it from the street. If their plot isn't in the shot, the video can't help with it.
- **It can't show status or prices.** And if it does, those details go out of date the day a plot sells, and changing them means editing the video again.
- **It shows one moment.** Drone footage shows the site on the day it was filmed. A 3D animation shows what is planned. Both are useful. Tell buyers which one they are watching: 3D homes are illustrations, not photos of the site.

## The interactive site plan

An interactive site plan is a version of the estate layout that buyers open from a link and explore themselves. Each plot can be tapped to show its details. Some are flat maps; some, like [Alfa Maps](https://maps.globalalfabet.com/), show the estate in 3D. We explain how ours is built from a layout plan in [Interactive site plans in 3D for property developers](/blog/interactive-3d-site-plan-for-property-developers).

**What it does well**

- **The buyer chooses what to look at.** They find their plot by number, or filter for plots that are still for sale, and look at the one they care about.
- **Status can stay current.** When your team marks a plot sold, the next buyer to open the link sees it. There is one place to update, not a stack of files.
- **It joins the facts together.** Size, boundary lengths, position, status, price and payment plans sit on the same plot, instead of in three documents.
- **It can show the place too.** A 3D plan shows the plot to scale, next to its neighbours, roads and open spaces, from above or from the street.

**Where it falls short**

- **It needs a connection.** It opens in a browser, so the buyer needs mobile data or Wi-Fi to explore it.
- **Someone has to keep it up to date.** Its biggest strength, current status, only holds if your team updates it when a plot sells. A stale interactive plan is no better than an old price list.
- **It is not the legal record.** It is built from the plan, but the approved or surveyed plan is still the document a buyer's lawyer checks.
- **It asks a little more of the buyer** than pressing play. A short guide on first opening helps.

## Which format answers which question?

Going back to the buyer's list:

- **Which plot, and is it still for sale?** Interactive site plan. A PDF shows the plot but not its status; a video may not show the plot at all.
- **How big is it, and what shape?** The layout plan is the record. An interactive plan built from it shows the same sizes and boundary lengths on the plot the buyer taps.
- **What is around it?** Video gives the feel; a 3D interactive plan lets the buyer look around their own plot.
- **What will it cost?** Interactive site plan, or a price list you keep current.
- **What could I build there?** Video or 3D, labelled as illustration.
- **Is it real and approved?** The approved layout plan, every time.
- **Who do I talk to?** Whatever you send should end with a name and one easy way to reach them.

## So which should you use?

For most estates the answer is all three, each doing its own job:

- **Use video to get attention.** Put it in adverts and social posts, and end it with a link to the estate.
- **Use the interactive site plan to help buyers choose.** It is where they find a plot, check it is still for sale, and work out what it costs.
- **Use the layout plan PDF to confirm.** Send it with the reservation and agreement, so the buyer and their lawyer can check the plot number, size and boundaries against the record.

If you only have time for one, think about where your buyers get stuck. If they never hear about the estate, start with video. If they hear about it but keep asking which plots are left and what they cost, an interactive site plan saves your team the most back-and-forth.

## In short

**How is an interactive site plan different from a PDF?**
A PDF layout plan is a fixed drawing: every plot looks the same, and it doesn't change when plots sell. An interactive site plan opens from a link, lets buyers tap a plot to see its size, status and price, and shows the latest status when the developer keeps it up to date.

**Is an interactive site plan better than a PDF layout plan?**
For choosing a plot, usually yes: it shows which plots are still for sale and puts each plot's facts in one place. For confirming a purchase, no: the approved layout plan is the record a buyer's lawyer checks.

**Is a video flyover enough on its own?**
Rarely. It shows the estate well, but buyers can't stop on their own plot, and it can't show which plots are sold.

**Do I need all three?**
Most estates benefit from each: video to get attention, an interactive site plan to help buyers choose, and the layout plan to confirm.
