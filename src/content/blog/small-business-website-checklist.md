A small business website needs to pass five checks. Open it on your phone and ask: does the first screen say what you do and for whom? Can a visitor reach you in one tap? Does it load fast on mobile data? Are your hours, prices and contact details still right? And can Google read it, with a clear title, a description and real text? Each check takes a few minutes, and none needs a new website.

The examples below come from our Studio library. They are concept work for fictional brands, designed to show each idea clearly. They are not real businesses.

## Check 1: Does the first screen say what you do, and for whom?

Open your home page on your phone and look only at the first screen, before you scroll. A stranger should be able to answer two questions from it: what do you do, and is it for them?

A good test is to show that screen to someone who doesn't know your business for five seconds, then ask what you sell. If they can't say, the words need work.

- **Lead with what you do, in plain words.** "Steel structures, designed and checked." says more than "Welcome to our website" or "Excellence since day one".
- **Add one short line on who it's for or what's included.** For example: "Warehouses, mezzanines and roof trusses."
- **Keep the logo and a big photo from pushing the words off the first screen.** On a phone, the words matter more than the picture.

See it on [Ferrovane](/studio/library/ferrovane), a concept site for a fictional steel structures engineer: the headline and one line under it say what the firm does before you scroll.

## Check 2: Can a visitor reach you in one tap?

Most people who want to call, message or book will not hunt for a contact page. Make the next step obvious and easy to press on a small screen.

- **Pick one main action** and name it for what happens: "Book a class", "Request a site survey", "Order a bouquet". One clear button beats five competing ones.
- **Keep it in reach.** Put it near the top of the page, and again at the bottom of the phone screen or in the menu, so it is never far away.
- **Make phone numbers and emails tappable.** A number written as plain text, or inside a picture, can't be tapped. Use a proper link, so one tap starts the call, the message or the email.
- **Make buttons big enough for a thumb.** Google's web.dev guide recommends tap targets of [around 48 device independent pixels, about 9 mm, with about 8 pixels between them](https://web.dev/articles/accessible-tap-targets).
- **Try it yourself.** Tap every contact link on your own phone and check where it goes. A form that sends to an old email address loses every enquiry without anyone noticing.

See it on [Brakko](/studio/library/brakko), a concept site for a fictional boxing gym: today's classes come first, each with its own Book button, and Book a class sits at the bottom of the phone screen.

## Check 3: Does it load fast on a phone?

A slow page loses visitors before they see anything. Test the page people land on most (usually the home page) on a phone, over mobile data, not office Wi-Fi.

To measure it, put your address into [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev), Google's tool for page speed. It reports the Core Web Vitals: three measures, defined by Google, of how a page feels to use. Google's web.dev guide sets a "good" score for each, [checked at the 75th percentile of page loads, with mobile and desktop measured separately](https://web.dev/articles/vitals):

- **Largest Contentful Paint (LCP): 2.5 seconds or less.** How long the main content takes to appear.
- **Interaction to Next Paint (INP): 200 milliseconds or less.** How quickly the page responds when someone taps.
- **Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS): 0.1 or less.** How much the page jumps around while it loads.

If the report says there isn't enough real-world data for your site, look at the lab test below it: it still shows what slows the page down.

The usual fixes are simple:

- **Shrink your photos.** A photo straight from a phone or camera is often far bigger than a page needs. Resize it to the size it is shown at, and save it in a modern format such as WebP.
- **Cut what plays on its own.** Autoplay videos, large sliders and pop-ups are heavy on mobile data.
- **Remove what you don't use.** Old plugins, chat widgets and tracking scripts each add weight.
- **Give images a set size,** so text doesn't jump when they load.

See it on [Brakko](/studio/library/brakko) again: it uses drawn shapes instead of photos, so the page stays light on mobile data.

## Check 4: Are your hours, prices and details current?

Wrong details can do more harm than missing ones. A customer who drives over to find you closed, or sees one price online and another at the counter, may not come back.

Go through the site with a list:

- **Opening hours,** including public holidays and any seasonal changes.
- **Phone number, email and address.** Call the number and send a test email.
- **Prices,** if you show them. If they change often, show fewer and keep them right, or say how to get today's price.
- **Products and services.** Remove what you no longer offer.
- **Team names, photos and dates.** A "latest news" box from two years ago makes a business look closed.

Then check that the same details appear everywhere people find you: your website, your Google Business Profile if you have one, and your social media pages. Put a reminder in your calendar to repeat the check every few months, and whenever something changes.

See it on [Petalune](/studio/library/petalune), a concept site for a fictional florist: the opening hours sit on the home page, with a line that says whether the shop is open now.

## Check 5: Can Google read it?

Google can only show your site to people searching for what you do if it can read and understand your pages. Four things matter most for a small site.

- **A clear title on every page.** The title is the blue link in search results. Google asks for [descriptive, concise titles, different on every page, without repeating keywords](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link). "Wedding flowers and bouquets | Petalune" works; "Home" does not.
- **A short description on every page.** Google writes the text under the link [mainly from the page itself, and sometimes uses your meta description](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) if it describes the page better. Write a different one for each page.
- **Real text, not text in pictures.** Your services, hours, area and contact details should be written as text on the page, not only inside images or PDF menus. Give each important image a short description (alt text), which [Google uses to understand the image](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images).
- **The same content on the phone as on the desktop.** Google [uses the mobile version of a site for indexing and ranking](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing?hl=en). If your phone layout hides services or contact details that the desktop shows, Google may miss them too.

To see whether Google has found your site at all, search Google for `site:` followed by your domain, for example `site:yourbusiness.com`. Google's own [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) explains this: if results from your site appear, it is in Google's index.

## Your checklist

Run this on your own phone, on mobile data:

1. The first screen says what you do and for whom, in one line.
2. One main button, easy to reach, and every phone number and email opens in one tap.
3. PageSpeed Insights shows your main page in the "good" range, or you know what to fix first.
4. Hours, prices, contact details and services are right, and match everywhere else you appear online.
5. Every page has its own title and description, the important words are real text, and `site:` search finds you.

## In short

**What should a small business website have?**
A first screen that says what you do and for whom, one clear way to get in touch, fast pages on a phone, current hours, prices and contact details, and a clear title, description and real text on every page.

**How fast should a small business website load?**
Google's web.dev guide counts a Largest Contentful Paint of 2.5 seconds or less as good, measured on mobile and desktop separately. Test your main page with PageSpeed Insights on a phone.

**How do I know if Google can find my website?**
Search Google for `site:` followed by your domain. If pages from your site appear, they are in Google's index.

**How often should I check my website?**
Whenever your hours, prices, services or contact details change, and every few months anyway.
